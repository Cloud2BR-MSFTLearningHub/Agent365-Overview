## Operating model objective

Create a repeatable model that connects platform, security, compliance, and
business teams for day-two operations.

## Role-to-process mapping

| Process | Primary owner | Supporting teams |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New agent onboarding | AI (artificial intelligence) admin | Platform engineering, security, compliance |
| Policy and exception review | Governance owner | Security operations, business owner |
| Runtime monitoring and triage | Security operations center | Platform support |
| Quality and value optimization | Product owner | Data analyst, platform engineering |
| Decommission and archival | AI (artificial intelligence) admin | Compliance and records owners |

## Cadence model

| Cadence | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | Incident triage, health checks, urgent fixes |
| Weekly | Policy exceptions, drift, and ownership review |
| Monthly | KPI, risk, and cost-performance review |
| Quarterly | Architecture, control, and standards refresh |

## Control artifacts

- Agent catalog with owner accountability.
- Risk register for active and pending exceptions.
- Runbook library for incidents and known failure modes.
- KPI scorecard with productivity and risk indicators.
- Audit trail for lifecycle and policy decisions.

## Success indicators

- Stable or improving reliability with growing adoption.
- Decreasing exception count or tighter exception aging.
- Reduced incident response time for known issue classes.
- Clear mapping between usage and measurable business outcomes.

## Business example

> A shared services team runs monthly operational reviews with security and
> compliance. It uses a joint scorecard for reliability, policy adherence, and
> value delivery. This shared cadence prevents siloed decisions and supports
> controlled scaling across business units.
